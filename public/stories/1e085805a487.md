# Author and family evacuate from Moscow to Samarkand

*1941*

Acting on his uncle R' Zusya's counsel to head for a 'Jewish city' rather than join his parents in Mozdok, the author dried bread into rusks, sold his cookware in Yegoryevsk, and on a Thursday in mid-Elul boarded a passenger train for the week-long journey to Tashkent with his wife, infant Leah, and sister Esther. The family spent Shabbat stranded in the Tashkent station park alongside refugees from Latvia, Lithuania, and Poland, then continued the twelve-hour ride to Samarkand. The author records that only in Tashkent did he begin to feel himself a refugee like the rest.
