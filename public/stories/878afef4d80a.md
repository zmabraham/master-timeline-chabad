# Senate ban on rabbinic fundraising and civil arbitration relayed

*1834*

Acting on Khovansky's 1827 recommendation, the Senate had referred the matter to the Minister of the Interior, who cited an 1828 directive forbidding new specific laws about Jews until the general Jewish statute was finalized. Instead, the minister called for strict enforcement of the existing 1804 statute's sections 49, 51, and 52. The Senate adopted this view in late January 1834, instructing Khovansky to notify Jews of Mohilev and Vitebsk that they must not donate to rabbis or use rabbis to settle civil disputes, but instead refer such matters to the established courts.
