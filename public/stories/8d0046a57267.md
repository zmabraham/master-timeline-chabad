# Three-way split on Russian rabbinic status at Petersburg Conference

*1910*

The central debate at the conference concerned the legal status of rabbis in Russia, who had not been government-recognized as spiritual leaders. The majority, led by Rabbi Chaim Ozer Grodzinski of Vilna, favored limited secular education and Russian language proficiency, with the rabbi also managing birth certificates. A twelve-member modernist minority led by the lawyer H. Sliozberg demanded broader secular education and refined Russian. An eight-member minority led by the Rebbe Rashab insisted that rabbis need only know Torah, with a separate clerk handling birth certificates. The conference elected a committee of three, including the Rebbe, to draft conclusions for the Interior Ministry's Religious Department.
