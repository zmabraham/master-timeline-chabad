# Author's wife hospitalized with typhus on Purim in Samarkand

*1942*

The first winter in Samarkand brought famine, unusual cold, and a typhus epidemic that filled the city's hospitals to capacity. Returning home from the Purim farbrengen, the author found his wife unwell; the next morning she was taken to hospital with typhus. Fearing the Soviet army press-gangs ('oblavy') that grabbed men off the streets and shipped them to the front, he could not even visit her — his sister Esther served as her contact until she returned home shortly before Passover.
